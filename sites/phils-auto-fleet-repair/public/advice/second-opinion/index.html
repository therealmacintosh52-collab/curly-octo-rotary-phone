Getting a second opinion on a repair quote

How to sanity-check an estimate before you spend the money.

When a second opinion is worth it

Not every quote needs one. If the repair is straightforward, the price is in a normal range and you trust the shop, get it done.

It is worth a second look when the number is large enough to matter, when the explanation didn't make sense to you, when a shop recommends replacing several parts to fix one symptom, or when work has already been done and the original problem is still there.

What to bring with you

  • The written estimate, with part names and labor hours if it lists them.
  • Any paperwork from work already performed on this problem.
  • The specific symptom in your own words — what it does, when it started, whether it happens cold, hot, at speed or at idle.
  • The codes, if anyone has read them for you.

Questions worth asking any shop

  • How do you know that's the failed part? A good answer describes a test. A weak answer describes a guess.
  • What happens if we replace it and the symptom stays? Shops that test before replacing can answer this comfortably.
  • Which of these items are safety, and which can wait? Any honest shop will separate the list for you.
  • Can you show me? Worn brake pads, a leaking gasket and a cracked belt are all things you can be walked out to see.

What we do with a second opinion

We start from your symptom, not from the previous shop's conclusion, because inheriting an assumption is how a misdiagnosis gets repeated. We test, we tell you what we find, and we tell you plainly when the first quote was right — that happens, and it is a perfectly good outcome. You leave knowing the number was fair.

The short version

Bring the estimate and the paperwork. We will test the vehicle ourselves and give you a straight answer, including when the other shop was right.
