# Review request templates

55 Google reviews at 4.5 is solid. The shops that outrank Automotive Solutions in
Elk Grove mostly do not do better work — they ask more often. This is the cheapest
ranking lever the business has.

**Rules that matter more than the wording:**

- Ask in person, at the counter, at handover. Response rates collapse over email alone.
- Ask the same day. Memory of the repair fades in 48 hours.
- Never offer anything in exchange for a review. Google removes them, and in the
  US the FTC treats incentivised reviews without disclosure as deceptive.
- Never filter — do not ask "were you happy?" first and only send the link to the
  people who say yes. Google calls that review gating and penalises it.
- One ask, one reminder. Never a third.

The QR code in `assets/review-qr.svg` (and `.png`) points at the Google listing.
**Replace it** with the short "write a review" link from the Google Business
Profile dashboard once you have it — that one opens the review box directly.
Print it at about 2 inches, on the counter card and on the invoice.

## At the counter (say it, do not read it)

> "Glad we got it sorted. If you've got a minute, a review on Google genuinely
> helps people find us — there's a QR code right here."

Then hand them the card and stop talking. Do not explain further.

## Text message — same day, after pickup

> Hi [first name], thanks for bringing the [make] in today — Automotive Solutions.
> If we did right by you, a quick Google review really helps a small shop like
> ours: [link]
> Anything not right, call me first on (916) 686-5277 and I'll fix it.

## Text message — single reminder, 3 days later

> Hi [first name], following up once — if you've got 30 seconds for a Google
> review it'd mean a lot: [link]. Either way, thanks for the business.

## Responding to reviews

Reply to every one, good and bad, within a couple of days. Google's own guidance
says responding helps, and it is the only part of your review profile you fully
control.

**Positive** — short, specific, no template smell:

> Thanks [first name] — glad the [repair] sorted it. Appreciate you making the
> trip from [town].

**Negative** — never argue in public. The audience is the next reader, not the
reviewer:

> I'm sorry that's how it went, [first name]. That isn't how we want anyone to
> leave. Please call me on (916) 686-5277 — I'd like to understand what happened
> and put it right.

Then actually call them.

**Do not:** quote policy, mention what they were charged, imply they are lying,
or reply with the same sentence to twenty people. All four are visible from orbit.

## Where else to ask, in priority order

1. **Google** — the only one that moves the map pack. 90% of the effort goes here.
2. **Yelp** — do not solicit directly; Yelp filters solicited reviews. Put the
   logo on the counter card and let it happen.
3. **NAPA AutoCare / SureCritic** — automatic if the shop uses NAPA's program.
4. **Nextdoor** — ask customers who mention they are local; neighbourhood
   recommendations convert unusually well for trades.
