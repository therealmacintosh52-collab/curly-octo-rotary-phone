### 2. Price-led vs trust-led; contact mode
- **Price-led entry, trust-led close.** Customers search "how much", but listings compete on "free estimate by text/phone" and fast turnaround [T01]. The text/photo quote is the dominant low-friction path in competitor copy (The Dent Tent: free estimates by phone or text, per directory listing). Inference: a photo-quote path (send 3 photos by text) matches how this is sold. Confidence Likely (listing text), not observed behavior data.
- **Call vs text vs book.** Not measurable from public data here. Do not claim a split. Recommend offering all three (tap-to-call, tap-to-text with photo prompt, short quote form) and measuring.
- **Trust signals buyers need** (inferred from what competitors foreground): years of experience, review count, "guaranteed", insurance claim help, makes/models. 916 has none of these supplied yet; they are TO-CONFIRM items, not copy.

### 6. Process, timeline, what PDR can and cannot fix
- Definition: technicians push or pull the dent from behind/around the panel; no filler or repaint (CARSTAR Sacramento page; Wikipedia general) [T01].
- Limits (CARSTAR local page): dents or creases that are too sharp, or paint that is stretched, may not be repairable [T02].
- Additional limits from general PDR articles (low authority, consistent with each other) [T03]: best for shallow, round dents with intact paint; deep or sharp creases are case-by-case; white stress marks suggest stretched metal; cracked or chipped paint points to conventional repair; aluminum work-hardens and needs careful assessment; panel back-access matters; structural damage and areas near airbag sensors are not DIY.
- Timelines (all competitor self-claims, NOT neutral): body shop PDR 2-3 days (CARSTAR) [T02]; specialist shops claim 30 min to 2 hours per job (Dent Tent, Dent Shoppe listings) [C-rows]. Hail with many dents is multi-hour to multi-day: UNVERIFIED, no source.
- Coating process: paint correction before coating because the coating seals in defects; initial cure about 24-48 h and full cure 7-14 days per one shop, product-specific [T15]. Do not publish cure rules except as "follow the product's data sheet".

### 7. Price DRIVERS (no 916 prices exist; none invented)
Dent size, depth and sharpness; number of dents and panels; panel material (aluminum is harder; State Farm's PDR matrix reportedly adds 25% for aluminum, prior research, not re-verified [T06]); access (roof rails, braced panels, edges); paint condition; oversized dents billed separately (State Farm matrix [T06]); mobile/travel fee [T18]; hail count and insurance estimating basis; for detailing: vehicle size, condition, add-ons, pet hair/stains, sap; for coating: paint correction hours, product tier and warranty; for B2B: volume and repeat.
**Market reference only (all low reliability; scraped/SEO pages; never publish as 916's prices):** small PDR dent about $75-$150, medium $150-$350, multi-dent on a panel $200-$400 (shop press release) [T08]; hail $500-$1,500 per one guide [T08]; mobile fee about $60 [T18]; mobile detailing about $75-$200 in a West Sacramento guide, Sacramento competitor anchors $30 starter wash and $120 package [T16]; professional ceramic $800-$3,000+ [T15]; dealer volume rates $30-$50 per dent from a 2020 blog [T17] (Unverified). Wholesale/dealer rate card: NOT FOUND.

### 8. Insurance and hail claim process (informational)
- Hail is typically a comprehensive-coverage claim; liability-only policies do not cover it; per-claim deductible commonly cited at $500-$1,000; if the repair is under the deductible, filing may not pay; total-loss possible if repair exceeds actual cash value [T04] (general insurance guides, not CDI).
- CDI (official) says comprehensive can apply to falling-debris/flood damage and gives the consumer hotline 800-927-4357 [T05]. No CDI hail-specific page found; no California-specific rule on hail deductibles found.
- Whether a hail claim raises premiums: sources disagree [T04]. Site must not state either way.
- Customer practice: photograph damage before and after, keep all communication [T04].
- The site should say "we provide a written estimate and photos for your claim"; it must not say it will "handle" or "negotiate" the claim (see L18).

### B1. Bureau of Automotive Repair (BAR) registration (OPEN, high stakes)
- Statute: B&P 9882/9880.1: an "automotive repair dealer" is a person who, for compensation, repairs or diagnoses vehicle malfunctions; it is unlawful to be an ARD unless registered (2015 bill analysis, Justia 2024 reprint) [L01]. A third-party BAR guide says registration applies to businesses paid to repair motor vehicles, including body repair, with limited minor-service exemptions (tires, bulbs, etc.) [L01]. 
- I did **not** find any text saying PDR-only or mobile detailing is exempt, and did not find any text saying it is covered. Detailing (cleaning/appearance) is not obviously "repair of malfunctions"; PDR (body repair) is the open question. Other states treat PDR differently (Ohio requires registration; a Minnesota bill drafted an exemption) and prove nothing for California.
- Confidence: Unverified. **Status: TO-CONFIRM.** Owner should contact BAR (bar.ca.gov, consumer line) with the exact question, in writing, before launch.
- Site rule: do NOT write "BAR registered", "state licensed" or show any registration number unless the owner holds one. If registration is obtained, BAR advertising and estimate rules (e.g. written-estimate requirements) apply: not searched, UNVERIFIED. If registration is required and absent, publishing PDR prices could itself be a problem; flag to attorney.

### B2. "Licensed and insured"
- No state license for auto detailing was found (blog sources, Unverified) [L09/L11]. Contractor-license statutes (B&P 7027 family) govern construction contractors and are not relevant to car work; do not use contractor-style language ("contractor", "licensed contractor", "bonded") [Unverified: statute not retrieved].
- City of Sacramento requires a Business Operations Tax certificate for businesses operating in the city; home-based operators also need a home occupation permit [L10] (cityofsacramento.gov, Likely). Rules for Elk Grove, Roseville, Folsom, Rancho Cordova, Citrus Heights, Davis, West Sacramento, Lincoln, Rocklin, El Dorado Hills (unincorporated) and Granite Bay (unincorporated) were NOT researched: UNVERIFIED. Whether serving a city requires that city's certificate if based elsewhere is unresolved (call Sacramento Revenue Division, 916-808-8500, per city page).
- **Must:** say "licensed" only with a named, checkable license/registration the owner holds. "Insured" only if a current policy exists and a certificate of insurance can be produced. Safer: "Certificate of insurance available on request" once true. **Must not:** "fully licensed and insured", "bonded", "certified" (including product-certified installer status, e.g. Ceramic Pro-type certifications), without proof. Logged to TO-CONFIRM.

### B3. Advertising law (false/misleading)
- B&P 17500 makes false or misleading advertising for goods/services a misdemeanor (up to 6 months jail and/or $2,500) with private restitution/injunction rights; B&P 17200 UCL borrows it; CLRA Civ. Code 1770 lists prohibited consumer practices with a 30-day demand letter (1782); damages, punitive up to $5,000 for willful [L02-L04]. Confidence Likely (statute sites and summaries; leginfo text not retrieved).
- **Must:** every result, time, comparison and "best/fastest" claim be true and substantiable. **Must not:** "best in Sacramento", "#1", "cheapest", "same day" or "30 minutes" unless true for the stated case; no fake urgency; no "free estimate" if there is any charge.

### B4. Price advertising
- SB 478 "Honest Pricing" (Civ. Code 1770(a)(29), effective 2024-07-01): an advertised price for consumer (personal-use) purchases must include all mandatory fees except certain government charges (and shipping for goods). Optional or later-action fees need not be included. Applies to goods/services for personal use; commercial purchases outside it. Clear-and-conspicuous disclosure standard applies [L05]. Confidence Likely/Verified (AG page plus law-firm summaries).
- **Implication:** a mandatory travel/mobile fee must be in any advertised retail price. Avoid "from $X" prices that exclude a fee you always charge. If travel fee depends on distance from 95624, state "travel fee based on ZIP, shown before you book" and show the total in the quote. Any "was/now" pricing needs a genuine prior price (former-price rules, B&P 17501: not retrieved, UNVERIFIED).
- **Must not** publish any price until the owner supplies it. Market-reference numbers in this file must not appear on the site as 916's prices.

### B5. Warranties and guarantees
- Civ. Code 1793.1: express warranties on consumer goods must be set out in simple, readily understood language identifying the warrantor (applies to goods; service guarantees still fall under UCL/CLRA) [L08]. FTC Guides 16 CFR 239: "lifetime"/"satisfaction guaranteed" claims must disclose terms clearly (239.3 satisfaction, 239.4 lifetime) [L07]; full text not retrieved: Likely.
- **Must:** if a guarantee exists, publish its written terms (what is covered, duration, exclusions, how to claim). **Must not:** "100% satisfaction guaranteed", "lifetime warranty", "warranty-backed coating" unless owner supplies terms. Competitors use "guaranteed" language [C-rows]; do not follow without terms.

### B6. Reviews and testimonials
- FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, 16 CFR Part 465, effective 2024-10-21: bans fake or fabricated reviews, buying reviews conditioned on sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, and certain review suppression [L06]. Verified (Federal Register plus law-firm summaries).
- **Must:** ask every customer for a review without screening by sentiment; show only real reviews with name/source/link; disclose any incentive. **Must not:** write, buy or curate fakes; hide negative reviews through gating; offer rewards tied to 5 stars. (Consistent with BLUEPRINT rules 2 and Stage 5.)

### B7. Texting and calling (TCPA / California)
- TCPA: marketing texts to wireless numbers need prior express written consent; informational texts are looser; consumers may revoke consent by any reasonable means (STOP, QUIT, END, REVOKE, OPT OUT, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE); honor within 10 business days at most; some revocation provisions were delayed by a waiver: current status UNVERIFIED [L12]. Confidence Likely.
- California-specific texting law (CEMA, B&P 22948 et seq.) and mini-TCPA rules: NOT researched: UNVERIFIED.
- **Must:** a consent line next to any phone field ("By submitting you agree to receive texts about your quote; msg/data rates may apply; reply STOP to opt out"), a working STOP/HELP, and no marketing texts without recorded consent. **Must not:** pre-check consent boxes or text customers' lists bought elsewhere. A quote-reply text to someone who texted first is generally informational (attorney to confirm).

### B8. Privacy (CCPA/CPRA, CalOPPA)
- CCPA applies to for-profit businesses meeting any one test: annual gross revenue over $26,625,000 (CPPA figure effective 2025-01-01, CPI-adjusted, next adjustment after 2025), or buying/selling/sharing personal info of 100,000+ California consumers/households, or 50%+ of revenue from selling/sharing personal info (the last two from secondary sources) [L13]. A local mobile operator likely meets none: Likely, not legal advice.
- CalOPPA (B&P 22575) privacy-policy requirement for sites collecting personal info: not searched: UNVERIFIED. 
- **Must:** publish a plain privacy policy describing the quote form data, analytics and texting. **Must not:** claim "CCPA compliant" or "we never share data" without basis; no hidden trackers.

### B9. Web accessibility

Midvale); California bills to codify WCAG 2.1 AA (AB 1757) stalled; DOJ's 2024 Title II rule covers government sites only [L14]. Confidence Likely; bill status UNVERIFIED.

### B10. Stormwater and water discharge (mobile washing)
- Sacramento County Code ch. 15.12: only stormwater and 18 listed exempt discharges may enter the storm drain; the residential-vehicle-washing exemption does not extend to commercial mobile washing; wash water with soaps/cleaners should go to the sanitary sewer; fines up to $5,000 per day per regional guidance (flyer, not ordinance text); each city (Sacramento, Elk Grove, Folsom, Citrus Heights, Rancho Cordova etc.) has its own similar ordinance [L15]. Confidence Likely. The excerpt was dated 2004: confirm current text. West Sacramento publishes mobile-cleaning guidance (prior research). No Sacramento mobile-washer permit was found (Contra Costa's Central San has one; it does not apply here).
- Free help: Business Environmental Resource Center (916) 649-0225 (prior research, not called).
- **Must:** describe water handling truthfully (e.g. "rinseless/waterless methods and no wash water to storm drains", only if that is the owner's real practice). **Must not:** claim "eco-friendly", "biodegradable", "zero discharge" or "green" without substantiation (FTC Green Guides, 16 CFR 260: not retrieved, UNVERIFIED). PDR uses no water; the claim "no water" is safe for dent removal only.
