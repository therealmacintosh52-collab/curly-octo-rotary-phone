# Dispatch service agreement — brief for your attorney

**This is not a contract and it is not legal advice.** It's a checklist of what your
agreement needs to address, written so you can hand it to a transportation attorney and
not pay them to interview you first. Budget an hour of their time.

Find someone who does **transportation/FMCSA work specifically** — the agent-versus-broker
distinction below is the whole ballgame and a general small-business lawyer will miss it.

---

## Why you can't just use a template off the internet

Most free "dispatch agreement" templates online are written for dispatchers operating as
unlicensed brokers, because a lot of dispatchers are. Signing one would contradict the
compliance section of your own website and undercut the exact thing you're selling.

---

## 1. The characterisation — the load-bearing clause

Your site states plainly, in the section headed *"We're your agent. Not a broker,"* that:

- Freight moves under the **carrier's** MC authority, never Steadhaul's
- Steadhaul holds **no broker authority**, does not solicit shippers, and does not arrange
  transportation between shippers and carriers
- Steadhaul acts as the **carrier's bona fide agent**, negotiating on the carrier's behalf
  with brokers who are already licensed and bonded

**The agreement has to say the same thing, and your operations have to match it.** FMCSA
has been active on unlicensed dispatch. Ask your attorney directly: *does this agreement,
as written, keep me on the agent side of the line, and is there anything in how I
actually operate that would undercut it?*

## 2. Money — never through you

- The broker pays the **carrier or the carrier's factoring company directly**
- Steadhaul never receives, holds, or disburses freight revenue
- Steadhaul invoices the carrier separately for the dispatch fee

This is also on the website. It needs to be in the contract.

## 3. Fees and the terms you've advertised

Every one of these is advertised publicly, which makes it enforceable. The contract
should match the website exactly, or change the website.

- **Flat weekly fee per truck** — $300 Core / $425 Pro / $550 Full Desk. Never a
  percentage. What each tier includes.
- **First week free.** Define precisely when week one starts — first load booked, or
  agreement signed? Ambiguity here is your first dispute.
- **Truck down, no charge.** "If we didn't book you a load that week, we don't bill you
  for that week." Define who tells whom, and by when.
- **No card on file to start.** When does the first invoice issue, and on what terms?
- **Week to week, 7 days' notice, either side.** No setup fee, no cancellation fee. The
  site says a carrier can leave "any Friday" — make sure the notice mechanics actually
  permit that.
- Fleet volume pricing for 3+ trucks, if you're offering it.

## 4. Load approval

Nothing books without the carrier's yes. Say what "yes" is — verbal on a recorded line,
text message, email? This matters the first time a carrier says they never agreed to a
load.

## 5. Capacity

The site promises a **hard cap of eight trucks per dispatcher**. Decide with your
attorney whether that belongs in the contract as a commitment or stays a published
service standard. Either is defensible; silently breaking it is not.

## 6. Carrier's obligations and warranties

- Operating authority is **active** and will stay active
- Insurance meets stated minimums, is active, and the carrier **notifies Steadhaul
  immediately** of any lapse, cancellation or material change
- Carrier holds all required permits and endorsements for what they accept
- Carrier maintains FMCSA compliance — safety rating, ELD, drug and alcohol program
- Carrier will not double-broker or re-broker loads sourced by Steadhaul

## 7. Data handling

Worth writing in, because you're doing better than the competition and should get credit:

- What documents Steadhaul collects and why
- That the **W-9 is deleted** once broker setups are complete
- That documents are collected only through an encrypted signing service, never by email
  or text
- That Steadhaul never requests bank account details
- Carrier consent to Steadhaul submitting their packet to brokers on their behalf

That last point is a real authorisation you need — you're transmitting their information
to third parties. California Civ. Code § 1798.81.5(c) also requires you to contractually
require reasonable security from third parties you disclose personal information to; ask
your attorney how that interacts with broker portals.

## 8. Liability

For your attorney to size, but at minimum:

- Steadhaul is not liable for broker non-payment, and does not guarantee it
- Steadhaul does not guarantee load volume, rates, or revenue *(your ads must not either
  — see `ADS.md`)*
- Steadhaul is not a party to the transportation contract between carrier and broker
- Cargo loss, damage and claims are between carrier, broker and their insurers
- Liability cap — commonly the fees paid in some preceding period
- Indemnification, both directions

## 9. The usual machinery

Term and renewal · termination for cause versus convenience · confidentiality both ways ·
whether there's a non-solicitation on the broker relationships you build · governing law
and venue (California, presumably Sacramento County) · dispute resolution — arbitration
or courts · assignment · entire agreement · severability · how amendments happen ·
e-signature validity (your carriers will sign electronically).

---

## Questions to actually ask them

1. Does this agreement, as drafted, keep me clearly on the **carrier's-agent** side of
   the FMCSA line — and is there anything about how I operate that would undercut it?
2. Am I exposed if a broker I vetted fails to pay a carrier?
3. Do I need my own **insurance** — errors and omissions, or contingent cargo? What do
   dispatch services in California typically carry?
4. I collect W-9s containing SSNs and delete them after broker setup. Does my retention
   practice satisfy **Civ. Code §§ 1798.81 and 1798.81.5**?
5. My website advertises "first week free," "truck down you owe nothing," "leave any
   Friday" and "eight trucks per dispatcher." Are those enforceable promises, and does
   the contract need to mirror them?
6. Should this be one agreement, or an agreement plus a rate schedule I can update
   without re-signing everyone?

## Where to find someone

The Transportation Lawyers Association and the California Trucking Association both have
member directories. A transportation attorney reviewing a short services agreement is
typically a few hundred dollars — cheap against the first dispute, and much cheaper than
an FMCSA problem over the broker/agent line.
