Compliance

We're your agent. Not a broker.

FMCSA has been tightening the screws on unlicensed dispatch, and you've probably heard about carriers getting burned by a "dispatcher" who was quietly acting as an unlicensed broker. It's a fair thing to worry about. So here is exactly how Steadhaul is structured:

Freight moves under your MC authority, never ours. We don't hold broker authority, we don't solicit shippers, and we don't arrange transportation between shippers and carriers. We act as your carrier's bona fide agent — a role FMCSA explicitly permits — negotiating on your behalf with brokers who are already licensed and bonded.

Broker→You (or your factoring company) Steadhaul is never in this chain.

We invoice you separately, for dispatch. A flat weekly fee, billed to you directly. That's the whole commercial relationship. We never take a cut of a rate confirmation, never hold your freight revenue, and never sit between you and the money you earned.

Ask any dispatcher you're considering to explain this in writing. The ones who get vague about it are the ones to walk away from.

Straight answers

Questions carriers ask

What does it cost?

One flat rate per truck per week: $300 Core, $425 Pro, $550 Full Desk. Never a percentage. Your first week is free, and if your truck doesn't roll that week, you owe nothing. Fleets of three or more get volume pricing.

Do you take a percentage of my loads?

Never. Gross $4,000 or $14,000 — the fee is identical. Every rate confirmation comes straight to you, so you always see exactly what the load pays. A percentage dispatcher's raise comes out of your best week; ours doesn't.

What if my truck is down for the week?

You owe nothing. Breakdown, home time, surgery, hurricane — if we didn't book you a load that week, we don't bill you for that week. Text your dispatcher and we pause the desk until you're rolling again.

Do I have to sign a contract?

No. Week to week. You can leave any Friday with seven days' notice — by text, if that's easiest. No setup fees, no cancellation fees, and no card on file to start.

My MC is under 90 days old — can you help?

That's specifically who we're built for. Most dispatchers treat new authority as a reason to charge you a higher percentage. We charge the same as everyone else and do the work instead: broker packets to 25+ brokers who onboard young authorities, your COI placed, your MC set up in their systems, and a list of the brokers who'll auto-reject you so you stop wasting calls.

How fast can I start hauling?

Most carriers are set up the same day they send documents, and booked on a load within 24 to 48 hours. The bottleneck is almost always how fast your insurance agent sends the COI.

Who gets the freight payment?

You do. The broker pays you or your factoring company directly. We never touch your freight money — we're your carrier's agent, not a freight broker, and we don't hold broker authority. See the compliance section above for exactly how that's structured.

How many trucks does my dispatcher handle?

Eight, maximum. When a desk is full, you go on the list. We'd rather make you wait a week than overload a dispatcher and take your money anyway — a dispatcher juggling thirty trucks is why your last one stopped answering the phone.

Which load boards do you use?

The major national boards, plus direct broker lanes we've built over time. You don't pay for any of those subscriptions — that's on us, and it's part of what the weekly fee covers. We keep the exact mix and the broker list to ourselves; working out where the good freight actually is took years, and it's a lot of what you're paying for.

What if I don't like a load?

Nothing books without your yes. You approve every load before we send the rate confirmation. Turn down as many as you want — we don't get paid more for booking you into something you'd regret.
