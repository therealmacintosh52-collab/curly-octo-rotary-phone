Before you drive onto a dealer's lot with a car you want to trade in, you can do a few things that make a difference to how it looks and how the appraisal feels. You can also waste money on things that do not. This guide sorts the two, with honest limits, because no amount of cleaning changes the mileage, the service history or what the market is paying for your model.

One caveat first: we cannot tell you what a dealer will offer, and we will not promise that a repair or a detail will raise the offer by any specific amount. Appraisals depend on the dealer, the car and the market. What prep can do is remove reasons to mark the car down and help it make a good first impression.

## Start with an honest walk-around

Before spending anything, walk around the car as if you were buying it. Do it in daylight, preferably in shade, and look at each panel with light running across it. Make a list:

- dents and dings, with their size and location,
- scratches, scuffs and paint chips,
- cloudy or yellowed headlights,
- stains, odors and wear inside,
- warning lights, tire condition and anything mechanical.

Then sort the list into things that are cheap and quick to fix, things that are worth fixing and things to leave alone. Dealers do their own inspection and will notice what you have not mentioned.

## What is usually worth doing

### Small dings and dents, if the paint is intact

A few door dings or a shopping-cart mark are the kind of thing an appraiser notices straight away, since it signals reconditioning cost. Paintless repair removes them without touching the paint, and a repainted panel can be a bigger issue than a dent because it signals prior bodywork. Our [paintless dent repair page](/services/paintless-dent-repair) explains what suits the method and what does not, and [the cost guide](/blog/dent-repair-cost-sacramento) explains how pricing works. Get a quote first: if the cost of repair is close to what the dealer would deduct, it may not be worth your time.

### A proper interior clean

The inside is where buyers and appraisers form quick impressions. A thorough vacuum, a wipe-down of every surface, clean glass and removal of obvious stains and clutter helps. Smells matter, since pet and smoke odors are hard to ignore. An [interior detail](/services/interior-detailing) handles this, though some odors and stains can only be reduced, and we will say so.

### A wash and a basic exterior clean-up

A clean, shiny car looks better cared for than a dusty one, and it lets you see what is really there. A hand wash, tires and wheels cleaned, and glass done properly are a sensible minimum.

### Headlights, if they are clearly yellowed

Cloudy headlights make a car look older. If yours are yellow or foggy and the lenses are otherwise sound, [restoration](/services/headlight-restoration) is usually inexpensive relative to a headlight assembly.

### Gather the paperwork

Bring your title or payoff information, registration, both keys or fobs, the owner's manual and any service records. Missing keys are a common deduction, and records show the car has been looked after.

## What is usually not worth doing

- **Major paint work.** A repaint for cosmetic reasons rarely pays for itself on a trade-in, and a repaint signals prior bodywork.
- **Ceramic coating.** A coating is a good investment if you keep a car. It is rarely recovered on a trade-in. See [when a coating makes sense](/blog/ceramic-coating-in-valley-heat).
- **Paint correction on an older car.** A light polish may help the look, but a full multi-stage correction is rarely repaid at appraisal.
- **Mechanical repairs you cannot fully see through.** Dealers can often do mechanical work cheaper than you can, so check the quote before you pay for repairs ahead of time.
- **Fixing deep creases, cracked paint or panel damage** that would need a body shop. Compare the repair cost with what a dealer would likely deduct.

## Order of operations

Do things in the sensible order so that you do not undo your own work.

1. Fix dents first, because repair can disturb surrounding paint and dust.
2. Do any polishing or paint work next.
3. Clean the interior.
4. Wash and finish the exterior last, as close to the appraisal as is practical.

## Selling privately versus trading in

Trade-ins are convenient, and private sales usually bring more money for more effort. Prep matters in both. If you sell privately, buyers will look hard at dents, interior condition and service records, and a clean, dent-free car photographs well. If you trade in, you are mostly avoiding markdowns. It is worth getting a few offers, including online ones, before deciding.
