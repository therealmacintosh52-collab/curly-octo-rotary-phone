If you search for what a dent costs to fix, you will find a lot of confident numbers. Most of them are averages across the whole country, or ads for a shop's lowest offer. The honest answer is that two dents that look similar in a photo can cost very different amounts, and the reasons are mostly predictable. This guide explains the factors, so you can judge a quote when you get one, from us or from anyone else.

We are not going to give you a table of dollar figures here. Real prices live on our [pricing page](/pricing) and are confirmed on inspection, and any number we invented for a blog post would be a number we could not stand behind. What we can do is show you what makes a price go up or down.

## The factors that set the price

Every credible quote for dent repair is built from the same handful of inputs.

- **Size.** A dent the size of a coin is a different job from one the size of a dinner plate. Larger dents need more passes and more time to bring up evenly.
- **Depth.** Shallow dents rise quickly. Deep dents have stretched the metal and take careful, staged work, and may not come out completely.
- **Location on the panel.** The flat middle of a door is the easiest place. A dent on a body line, near an edge, or over a brace is harder because the metal is stiffer or the tools cannot reach as easily.
- **Material.** Steel is forgiving. Aluminum, used on hoods, doors and fenders of many newer vehicles, is stiffer and cracks more readily, so it takes more care and time.
- **Number of dents.** Ten dents on one panel are not ten times the work of one, but they are more than one. A hail-damaged car is priced by the panel and the count.
- **Paint condition.** If the paint is cracked, the dent is no longer a pure paintless job. Paint repair is a separate cost.
- **Access.** Some panels need a trim piece, a light or a headliner removed before the technician can reach behind the dent. That adds time.

## Why a photo is worth more than a phone guess

A phone description such as "a dent about this big on my door" leaves out almost everything above. A set of clear photos shows the size, the depth, the location and the paint condition at once. That is why we ask for pictures first, and why our [photo quote](/book#photo) exists. We reply with an estimate range, not a fixed number, because a photo cannot show everything. The final price is confirmed when we see the car in person, and we tell you before we start if anything has changed.

## How to compare quotes fairly

If you collect two or three quotes, make sure you are comparing the same thing. Ask each shop these questions.

1. **Is it paintless?** If a quote involves filler, sanding or paint, it is a different repair with different long-term behavior. Our [paintless dent repair page](/services/paintless-dent-repair) explains the method.
2. **Is it per dent, per panel or per car?** Methods of pricing differ. Per-dent pricing can add up quickly on a hail car, and per-panel pricing can be a better deal, or not.
3. **What happens if the dent cannot be fully removed?** A good shop tells you in advance when a result is likely to be partial, and explains what you will pay in that case.
4. **Is there a travel fee or a minimum?** Some shops have a minimum charge, so a single small ding can cost more than expected.
5. **When do you pay?** We take payment after you have seen the result, not before.

A very low quote is not necessarily a bargain. It can mean the shop plans to fill and repaint, that the quote will grow on inspection, or that the technician is less experienced. A quote that is higher than the others can reflect more careful work or a more difficult dent. The difference is easiest to judge when each shop has seen the same photos.

## When a repair is not worth it

Sometimes the right answer is to leave a dent alone. If a car is old, the dent is small and you do not plan to sell, spending money on cosmetics may not make sense. Equally, if the paint is cracked and the dent is deep, a paintless repair may only improve it partly, and a body shop may be the better route. We will tell you this directly. Our post on [PDR versus a body shop](/blog/pdr-vs-body-shop) walks through how to decide, and [can every dent be fixed without paint](/blog/can-every-dent-be-fixed-without-paint) explains the limits.

## What about insurance?

If the damage came from hail, a falling branch or another covered event, your comprehensive coverage may apply, depending on your policy and deductible. For a small ding that you caused yourself in a parking lot, the repair often costs less than a typical deductible, so a claim may not make sense. Check with your insurer. We can provide photos and an estimate to support a claim, and we do not tell customers what their policy covers.
