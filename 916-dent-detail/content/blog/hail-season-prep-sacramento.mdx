Hail is not something most Sacramento drivers plan for, and that is exactly the problem. When a storm does pass through, it tends to hit a lot of cars at once, and the people who were not prepared scramble. This guide is about what you can do in advance, what to do when a storm is coming, and what to do afterward, including how to document damage.

## How often does hail actually happen here?

Less often than in hail-prone parts of the country, but it does happen. A National Weather Service meteorologist in Sacramento has described a hail hit on the main metro area as relatively unusual, though accumulation of small hail in the Valley is not rare. In February 2018, local outlets reported hail piling up to as much as three inches in places around Sacramento. In March 2023, a severe thunderstorm warning flagged quarter-size hail and possible vehicle damage for areas south and east of the city. Local news has also reported hail in Roseville, Citrus Heights and Natomas in past storms, and a carport in Natomas collapsed in one of them.

We want to be careful here. We do not have a reliable count of how often hail has dented cars in the region, and the accumulating small hail of 2018 is not necessarily the kind that dents panels. The honest summary is: occasional, uneven and hard to predict. That is a reason to have a simple plan, not a reason for alarm.

## Before a storm: what actually helps

**Cover is the best protection.** If hail is forecast, move the car into a garage if you can. A solid roof beats any cover. A carport helps against sun and rain, but is no guarantee against large hail, and a failing structure can add damage of its own.

**Look for a parking structure.** If you do not have a garage, a multi-level parking structure at work or a store is a good short-term choice when a warning is issued.

**Use soft protection carefully.** Thick blankets, moving pads or purpose-made hail covers can reduce damage from small hail. They are not a guarantee against large stones. A loose cover can also scratch paint in wind, and wet covers trap moisture, so it is a trade-off. Never put a cover on dirty paint.

**Know your coverage.** Hail is usually handled under comprehensive coverage, not collision. Find out what your deductible is before a storm, not after. Some policies have a separate, higher deductible for weather events.

**Photograph your car now.** A set of dated photos of the car in good condition, taken from all sides and the roof, is useful evidence of its prior state. It takes five minutes.

## During a storm

Do not go outside to move the car while hail is falling. A dented panel is cheaper than an injury. Stay inside until the storm passes. If you are driving, find cover such as a parking structure or a gas-station canopy, and avoid stopping under overpasses where you could block traffic.

## After a storm: the first 48 hours

1. **Check the car in good light.** Hail dents are easiest to see on an overcast day or in shade, with light running across the panel. Look at the roof, hood, trunk lid and the tops of the fenders.
2. **Photograph everything.** Take wide shots of each panel and close-ups of representative dents. Include the date. Photograph other damage too, such as cracked glass or damaged trim.
3. **Do not wash or polish yet.** It is fine to rinse, but avoid anything that might hide or alter damage before it is documented.
4. **Call your insurer if you plan to claim.** Ask about their process, deadlines and whether they use their own appraisers. You are generally free to choose your repairer.
5. **Get an estimate.** We can [photograph and estimate by panel](/services/hail-damage-repair) to support a claim, and give you a repair route that does not need repainting where the paint is intact.

## Claims, in plain terms

Whether to claim depends on the extent of damage and your deductible. If repair costs are well above the deductible, a claim usually makes sense. If they are close to or below it, paying out of pocket can avoid a claim on your record, although practices vary by insurer. If the damage is severe enough that repair approaches the value of the car, an insurer can declare it a total loss, which is a decision made between you and the insurer. We are not insurance advisors, and we will not tell you what your policy covers.

A written estimate by panel, with photos, is useful whichever way you go.

## Which repairs are possible?

Hail dents are typically round and shallow with intact paint, and that suits paintless repair, panel by panel, with no repaint. It is not always the right method. Cracked paint at the point of impact, very deep dents, damage on panel edges, and heavily dented aluminum may need other approaches. Our article on [whether every dent can be fixed without paint](/blog/can-every-dent-be-fixed-without-paint) explains the limits, and [PDR versus a body shop](/blog/pdr-vs-body-shop) helps you think about the alternatives.

Time varies a lot. A car with light damage might be a day, while a heavily hit car could take several. We give you a realistic expectation after we have seen the damage in person.

If you manage a fleet or a dealer lot, plan before the season. Know where vehicles can be moved, who has the authority to move them and who will document damage. See our [dealership recon page](/services/dealership-recon) for how on-lot work is organized.

- Know your comprehensive deductible.
- Take dated photos of your car in good condition.
- Know where the nearest garage or parking structure is.
