People ask how long a mobile detail takes more than almost any other question, and it is a fair one, because the car is unavailable while we work and you want to plan your day. The frustrating truth is that no single number is right for every car. The better answer is a way of thinking about it, so you can estimate the time yourself and know when a quote makes sense. We are deliberately not publishing a table of exact hours here, since we do not want to promise a number that a muddy truck or a neglected cabin will blow through.

## What actually takes the time

Detailing is slow because it is methodical. A few things dominate.

**The scope.** A maintenance wash is short. A full interior and exterior detail takes many hours. Add paint correction or a ceramic coating and you are looking at a long day or more, sometimes with a second visit. Our [mobile detailing page](/services/mobile-detailing) and [interior detailing page](/services/interior-detailing) list what each includes.

**The size of the vehicle.** There is simply more surface on a three-row SUV or a full-size truck than on a compact car, and it takes proportionally longer to wash, decontaminate, polish and vacuum.

**The condition.** This is the biggest variable. A car that is washed regularly and has a tidy cabin goes quickly. A car with heavy pet hair, set-in stains, baked-on sap, thick brake dust or months of road film takes much longer. We would rather tell you that on the phone than discover it on arrival.

**The finish you want.** Machine polishing to remove swirl marks is slow, careful work. A quick wax is not. Each level of correction takes real time. See [paint correction](/services/paint-correction) for how that process runs.

**Conditions on the day.** Heat, wind, shade and access all matter. In summer we often work early in the day, because polish, wax and coatings behave badly on a hot panel. Rain affects exterior work. A garage makes everything more predictable.

## How the visit is structured

A typical full detail follows a sequence, and the order is part of why it takes time.

1. **Walk-around and plan.** We look at the car with you, note existing damage and agree what is included.
2. **Interior first.** Dry cleaning, vacuuming and compressed air from the top down, then wet cleaning by material, then glass.
3. **Wheels and tires.** These are the dirtiest part of the car, so we do them before touching paint.
4. **Wash and decontamination.** A careful hand wash, then a clay treatment to lift bonded grit.
5. **Correction, if included.** Machine polishing, panel by panel.
6. **Protection.** Wax, sealant or coating applied and, where needed, left to cure.
7. **Final inspection.** We check panels under light and the cabin in daylight, with you if you are there.

Skipping steps saves time but costs quality. A detail done in a hurry tends to leave dust in the vents, streaks on the glass and water spots on the paint.

## Planning your day around a visit

Book for a day when you can be flexible about the car. Tell us the make, model, condition and anything specific you want. We will give you a time window and tell you early if we think the job needs more. Most customers are not present for the whole visit.

- **At home.** You can usually carry on with your day. We need access to the car and, ideally, an outdoor tap and outlet, and we will plan around it if you have neither. Tell us when you book.
- **At work.** Check with the property first. Some buildings restrict water or outside contractors. If the car is in a garage, tell us the level and any pass requirements.
- **Combining services.** Doing a dent repair, a detail and a headlight restoration in one visit is often more efficient than three separate trips, though the total time is the sum of the work.
- **Several vehicles.** Two cars at one address can be sequenced so that we are not waiting on cure times. Tell us when you book.

## What does not speed it up

A few things people hope will shorten a visit usually do not. Rushing the decontamination step leads to scratches during polishing. Skipping the dry vacuum before wet cleaning moves dirt around. Using a lot more product does not make cleaning faster. A well-organized technician with the right tools is quick because of preparation, not shortcuts.

## How you can help shorten it

- Remove personal items and trash from the cabin before we arrive.
- Tell us about any child seats that need to come out or stay in.
- Park where there is room to open all the doors, ideally in shade.
- Send photos of the worst areas ahead of time.
- Let us know about gate codes, parking rules or access issues.

## Honest limits on estimates

We give a window, not a promise carved in stone. If we find something unexpected, such as heavy staining or contamination that was not visible in your photos, we tell you before we continue and give you the choice of adding time or leaving it. This is part of why we confirm scope at the start of each visit. Our post on [whether a mobile detailer is legit](/blog/is-a-mobile-detailer-legit) covers what to expect from any professional.

## The bottom line

Expect a real chunk of time for a full detail, much less for a maintenance clean and more for correction or coating.
