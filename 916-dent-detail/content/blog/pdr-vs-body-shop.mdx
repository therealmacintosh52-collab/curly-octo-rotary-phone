When you have a dent, you have two broad routes. You can have it repaired paintlessly, by working the metal back from behind, or you can take the car to a body shop for conventional repair with filler and paint. Neither is better in every case. This post compares them honestly, including where a body shop is the better choice, which is a thing a PDR company does not always tell you.

## How each method works

**Paintless dent repair (PDR).** A technician reaches behind the panel and uses rods and specialty tools to push the metal back into shape, in small steps, while watching reflections on the surface. The paint is never touched. See our [paintless dent repair page](/services/paintless-dent-repair).

**Conventional body repair.** The panel is sanded to bare metal or primer around the dent, the dent is pulled or hammered out as far as possible, body filler smooths the rest, and the area is primed, painted, blended into surrounding paint and clear-coated. It is a multi-step process with curing and drying time between steps.

## Comparing them on the things that matter

### Factory finish

PDR keeps your original paint. A repaint is a new finish, and while a good shop can match color very well, a perfect match in color, texture and thickness is difficult. Some buyers and inspectors can detect a repainted panel with a paint-thickness gauge, which can matter on a vehicle you plan to sell or return on a lease.

### Time

PDR is often a shorter job, because there is no filler, paint or curing. A body shop repair usually involves leaving the car for days. Some body shops say their own PDR work takes two to three days, so timelines vary by shop and workload. Mobile PDR, where the technician comes to you, avoids the drop-off entirely.

### Cost

The factors that set cost differ. PDR is priced by size, depth, location, material and number of dents, with no materials or paint to pay for. Body work involves labor, materials and paint, and typically costs more for the same dent, particularly where paint blending is needed. The exact relationship depends on the dent, and we will not quote a ratio. Our post on [what affects dent repair cost](/blog/dent-repair-cost-sacramento) explains the pricing inputs.

### Range of damage that can be fixed

This is where body shops win. A body shop can repair almost any dent, including those with cracked paint, sharp creases, damaged edges and deep stretches. PDR is limited to damage where the paint is intact and the metal can be returned. Our guide to [whether every dent can be fixed without paint](/blog/can-every-dent-be-fixed-without-paint) lists the limits.

### Long-term behavior

A PDR repair has no paint to fade or peel. A properly done body repair can last a long time too, but poor preparation, cheap filler or a poor paint match can show later as bubbling, cracking or color differences. Quality varies by shop in both trades.

### Convenience

Mobile PDR comes to your home or office. A body shop requires a drop-off and pick-up, and often a rental car.

## A quick decision guide

<Callout title="Choose PDR when">
The paint is intact, the dent is round or a shallow crease, it is in the middle of a panel, and you want to keep your factory finish. It is also a good choice for lease returns, trade-ins and newer cars.
</Callout>

<Callout title="Choose a body shop when">
The paint is cracked, chipped or scraped through, the dent is a sharp crease or on a panel edge, the panel has been filled before, or the damage is large and severe. Also if the damage includes structural or mechanical issues, which are not a PDR matter at all.
</Callout>

There is also a middle path. Sometimes we can remove most of a dent paintlessly, and a body shop can finish a small remaining area with minimal paint, which reduces the work on the shop's side. We will tell you if that is realistic for your car.

## Insurance and body shops

If the damage is part of a larger claim, such as an accident involving several panels, the insurer usually works with a body shop, and PDR may play a part for some panels. For a hail claim, PDR is often an accepted route if the paint is intact. Either way, you generally have a right to choose where the car is repaired, though processes differ by insurer, so check yours. For hail specifics, read our [hail damage page](/services/hail-damage-repair) and the guide to [hail season prep](/blog/hail-season-prep-sacramento).

## Questions to ask any shop

1. What method will you use, and why is it right for this dent?
2. What happens if the dent cannot be fully removed?
3. Is the quote fixed or an estimate, and what could change it?
4. How long will I be without my car?
5. If the panel is repainted, how will the color be matched and blended?
6. When do I pay?

A shop that answers these plainly is a good sign, whichever trade it is in.

## Our honest take

For the common dents, door dings, cart marks, small hail dents and minor creases, PDR is usually the better value because it is quicker and keeps your original paint.
