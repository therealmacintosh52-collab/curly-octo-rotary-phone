## 1. Checklist: what to look at (10 min, tick as you go)
A. Google Business Profile completeness (Maps card)
- [ ] Primary category fits the main service. [ ] Phone, hours, address or service area present.
- [ ] Services list / products filled. [ ] Description written. [ ] Website + booking/message link present.
- [ ] Hours look current (holiday/special hours if relevant).
Why it matters: Google says local results rest mainly on "relevance, distance, and prominence" and that accurate, complete info helps a business show up more often.
 VERIFIED(https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091, 2026-10-12, search-result summary: "local rankings rest mainly on relevance, distance, and prominence"; "accurate, complete, and engaging").
 Google gives no weights per factor; third-party weightings are opinion. HYPOTHESIS that "complete" profiles convert better; test with client numbers.
B. Reviews
- [ ] Count and average (read from the card). [ ] Date of the newest review. [ ] Owner replies: how many of the last 10 have a reply?
Why: Google lists review count and score under prominence, and says replying shows you value feedback.
 VERIFIED(https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091, 2026-10-12, summary: review count and score feed prominence; "replying to reviews shows that you value feedback").
 VERIFIED(https://support.google.com/business/answer/9075681, 2026-10-12, summary: "The person who wrote the review is notified when you reply").
 Google does NOT frame reviews as a direct ranking lever in its own tips (same summary). Pitch them as trust + visibility, never as "reviews = rank".
C. Photos
- [ ] Owner-uploaded photos present (storefront, work, team). [ ] Newest photo date. [ ] Logo/cover present.
 VERIFIED(https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091, 2026-10-12, summary: Google suggests photos and videos "to show customers what you offer"). Any "X photos = Y% more clicks" stat found online is UNVERIFIED; do not quote it.
D. Website presence
- [ ] Has a site? [ ] Loads on your phone in a few seconds (your eyes, not a tool). [ ] Phone number tappable. [ ] Says city + service. [ ] Clear next step (call/book).
 HYPOTHESIS: a missing or broken site loses calls. Not a ranking claim. Record only what you saw.
E. Top-3 competitors' cards
- [ ] Search "[trade] [city]", note the first 3 Maps results (skip ads). For each: review count, average, newest review, photo count (rough), has site.
- [ ] Note the gap: where the prospect is behind the median of the three.
F. AI-search answer check
- [ ] In Google (AI Mode/Overview if it shows) and one chat assistant you already have free access to, ask: "best [trade] in [city]" and "who does [service] near [neighborhood]". Note: is the business named? Which businesses are?
 VERIFIED(https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features, 2026-10-12, summary: "no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary"; lists keeping Business Profile info up to date as a best practice). So never promise "we get you into AI answers".
 HYPOTHESIS: answers vary per person and per day; treat it as a snapshot, label it "snapshot on [date]". Vendor claims on how often AI answers appear for local searches conflict (about 8% vs 68%) and are UNVERIFIED; do not quote.

## 2. Light rubric (owner's scoring rule, HYPOTHESIS — tune after 10 audits)
🟢 matches or beats the median of the 3 competitors / complete. 🟡 close or partly done. 🔴 clearly behind or missing.
Reviews: 🟢 count near competitors and newest review under 30 days, replies on most; 🔴 under half the competitors' count, or newest review over 6 months old, or no replies.
