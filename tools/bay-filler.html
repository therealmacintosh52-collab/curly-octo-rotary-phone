Adjust the assumptions
15%

Trade sources commonly cite ~20% with timely, personal follow-up. Defaulted low on purpose — if the owner pushes back, drag it down and the number still holds.

30%

The rest already booked with whoever answered. Weeks are counted at 4.33 per month.

10%

Automated 48-hour and day-of reminders with one-tap confirm typically land shops in the 8–12% band.

40%

A no-show is not automatically lost revenue — many reschedule. Only this share is genuinely gone.

7%

Win-back campaigns to lapsed lists commonly book 5–10%. Counted once, not monthly — do not present it as recurring.

02

The follow-up sequence

Fill one declined ticket in. It writes the three texts. Send from the shop's number, never yours.

03

Running it

Three touches over sixteen days. Stop the moment they reply — a live conversation beats the next scheduled message.

Day 0
Pull the list
Export declined recommendations from the last 90 days out of Tekmetric, Shopmonkey, AutoLeap, Mitchell1 or Shop-Ware. Drop anything under $150 and anything already serviced.
Day 1
Reminder
Name the vehicle and the exact service. No offer, no discount. You are reminding them of a decision they already made, not selling.
Day 6
Safety framing
Why it matters now, in plain language. This is the one that converts — it changes the deadline from "someday" to "before something else breaks."
Day 16
Close the file
A clean exit that gives them an easy out. Reliably pulls a last cluster of replies, and it ends the sequence without nagging.

Use their numbers, not the defaults. The prefilled figures are placeholders for demonstrating the sheet, not claims about any shop. Industry-wide statistics on declined-service recovery and missed calls circulate mostly from dealership service departments and from vendors selling call-answering software — they run far higher than an independent three-bay shop and will get you written off if you quote them across a desk. The defaults here are set deliberately below those published figures.
